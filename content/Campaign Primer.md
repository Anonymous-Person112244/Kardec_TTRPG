## 2018. Suburban Georgia. Something Is Wrong.

The setting is familiar: middle-class neighborhoods, strip malls, and maybe a Waffle House. Then something changes, and there are things in the woods that shouldn't exist.

**This is a survival game in a world that makes sense.** Creatures eat, hunt, nest, and migrate. Communities need food, tools, and medicine, and they pay well for what they lack. Learn how the world works, and you can take advantage of it. Play it right, and you get rich.

**Resources are limited, and keeping track of them is part of the game.** Food, weight, ammunition, and the condition of your gear all matter. A group that plans well goes further.

> [!info] The feel
> 
> Think Delicious in Dungeon (Dungeon Meshi) more than an action movie. Monsters are animals, with diets, habits, and weak spots. Dungeons work like ecosystems. Prices go up and down for a reason.

> [!example]+ A week out there
> 
> - You're down to two days of food. You set snares along a game trail and check them at dawn.
> - Something in the creek collects shiny things. You trade it a bottle cap for the watch it's carrying.
> - The community at the crossroads is short on salt. It pays three times the usual price.
> - Everyone says to stay away from the old quarry. You learn what lives there, what it eats, and when it sleeps. Then you go in.
> - What you bring back pays for a water filter, and the community finally has clean water.

## What You Do

The game has three pillars:

|Pillar|Share|In short|
|---|---|---|
|**Exploration**|~37%|Get there, find things, and stay supplied|
|**Combat**|~37%|Survive fights and know when to avoid them|
|**Social**|~26%|Talk your way past, through, or out of trouble|

Under all three, you manage supplies. What you carry, eat, and trade decides how far you get.

### Exploration

- **Get past obstacles.** Can you cross the balance beam to reach the loot on the other side? Can you climb the fence before the dogs get to you? Most obstacles need only one skill roll, and a failed roll can hurt.
- **Find your way.** You cross the wilderness and go into dungeons, places where you shouldn't be. You can get lost.
- **Learn how things live.** Creatures have diets, habits, and tracks you can follow. What you learn helps you hunt them, avoid them, or use them.
- **Scavenge and harvest.** Houses, garages, and gas stations all have something useful, if you have time to look. So does a creature you bring down: meat, hide, and whatever it was carrying.
- **Track your gear and food.** Encumbrance (how much your load slows you down) and food are at the center of the game. A group that runs out of food doesn't last long. Neither does a group with too much gear to outrun anything.

### Combat

- **Fights are fast, ugly, and dangerous.** One bad hit can knock someone out of the fight.
- **The smartest fight is often the one you avoid.** You can run, hide, or barricade the door. All of these are good choices.
- **Know what you're fighting.** A creature's habits and weak spots matter as much as your weapon.
- **Use what you have.** Improvised weapons, cover, and the terrain can turn a bad fight into one you can survive.
- **Wounds last.** Injuries take a long time to heal, and someone has to treat them.

Survival matters more than flashy tactics.

### Social

- **Talk your way through.** Get past a roadblock, a cop, or a scared neighbor with a shotgun.
- **Trade.** Every community needs something, and the worse a shortage gets, the more it pays. Find out what a community needs, and you can get rich.
- **Change things.** Supply a community, and it grows and makes more. You can also hurt one: burn its grain, or foul its well. Both are your choice, and both have consequences.
- **Read people.** Is the stranger at the door honest?
- **Decide who to trust.** Who do you take in, and who do you leave behind?

> [!info] What if something stranger were possible?
> 
> Suppose, hypothetically, that some people could do things that shouldn't be possible. For example:
> 
> - An engineer's homemade net launcher pins a charging attacker at 20 yards.
> - A neighbor always seems to know where trouble will come from.
> - Someone leaves their body behind and looks into the next room.
> - A few words and a line of salt keep something out of the house.
> 
> Even the strange things follow rules, and they cost something. If that sounds like your kind of character, there's a path for it. See [[Creating a Character]].

## How Hard Is This to Learn?

We use _**GURPS**_ _Fourth Edition_. People say it's complicated, and the rulebooks are long, dense, and detailed. **Don't read them.** They overwhelm even experienced players, and this vault replaces them.

Playing is simpler than it looks. Almost every action uses the same roll: **you roll three dice (3d) against your skill.** The situation can raise or lower that skill with a bonus or a penalty.

The game has a lot of depth: hit locations, damage types, tactical maps, and more. We don't start there. We add new rules as the group gets comfortable. If combat takes up the whole session and nobody has fun, we can simplify it.

### The One Mechanic

When an action can fail, you roll three six-sided dice and add them together. _**GURPS**_ writes this as **3d**. To succeed, the total must be **equal to or less than** your skill. **Lower is better.** If you know _**D&D**_, this is backward from what you're used to.

Skills use the format **name-number**. **Scrounging-12** means the Scrounging skill at level 12. This table shows what those numbers mean:

|Skill|Chance to succeed|Meaning|
|--:|--:|---|
|**10**|**50%**|Hobbyist, shaky|
|**12**|**74%**|Competent, a good target|
|**15**|**95%**|Expert, elite|

Most professionals have **12** or **13** in their main skill. A typical paramedic has **First Aid-12** or **13**. A war-zone trauma doctor has **15**.

> [!example]+ Scavenging a garage
> 
> You search a garage in a half-evacuated neighborhood before danger catches up to you. You have **Scrounging-12**.
> 
> 🎲 You roll: 4 + 3 + 2 = **9**
> 
> 9 ≤ 12: **Success!** Behind a lawnmower, you find a machete (still sharp), a hockey helmet, shoulder pads, and a pair of shin guards. It isn't real armor, but it's better than nothing.
